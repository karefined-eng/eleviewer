## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: This interface feels authored for a study workspace rather than a generic desktop app. The product language—course folders, bookmarks, recent files, split-screen research, Quick Note—gives it clear identity. That said, the current welcome screen is not yet distinctive enough to feel confident. It reads like a tidy but generic launcher/dashboard with a strong product brief behind it, rather than a world that clearly teaches users what EleViewer is for.

**Deterministic scan**: No formal automated detector was available in this session, so this assessment is based on source review and product context rather than a CLI scan. The relevant home screen logic is concentrated in `ui.py`, particularly `_create_welcome_widget()`. The app does show product-specific intent through the “course folder,” “Open the web panel,” and “Quick note” flows.

## Overall Impression

EleViewer has a credible core idea and a strong product story. The welcome screen does the right things at a conceptual level, but it still behaves like a dashboard in search of a clearer user journey. Students are likely to understand the product once they start using it, but the first launch experience still asks them to infer too much.

## What’s Working

- The app clearly speaks the language of a study workflow. “Course folder,” “Recent files,” “Bookmarks,” and “Open the web panel” are direct product cues, not generic app filler.
- The quick actions are useful and well-selected for the product’s core value: open a file, start a note, keep research open, and capture a quick thought out of context.
- The visual system is restrained and readable. The palette and panel treatment are calm and fit a study environment rather than a consumer brand splash screen.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Welcome screen overload**: What: The first tab stacks a hero section, quick-start actions, an omnibar, and two information panels. Why it matters: newcomers must triage several competing calls to action at once, which creates cognitive load before any real work begins. Fix: Reduce the home to one dominant primary action, then surface secondary tools as lower-contrast panels. Suggested command: `$impeccable distill` or `$impeccable layout`

- **[P1] First-run setup is too implicit**: What: “Add course folder” appears as a link, but there is no explicit guided setup when the user has no vaults. Why it matters: a first-timer may not understand the most important prerequisite to the product. Fix: Add a clear empty-state CTA and a short onboarding step that explains the value of linking a course folder. Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`

- **[P2] Search affordance is conceptually helpful but operationally fuzzy**: What: The omnibar mixes file-name lookup and web URL detection in one field without clearly indicating the system’s mode. Why it matters: users are asked to guess whether they are searching local files, a web address, or a course folder. Fix: Separate “local search” and “open URL” modes or add explicit labels and visual mode switching. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

- **[P2] Empty states are functional but not reassuring**: What: “No recent files” and “No bookmarks” are technically fine, but they do not tell the user what to do next. Why it matters: empty states should feel like progress, not a dead end. Fix: Replace bare empties with action-oriented guidance and a next-step CTA. Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard` or `$impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User)**: The app shows quick actions and keyboard shortcuts, which is promising, but the default welcome screen is still heavy and mouse-first. The main value is clear, yet the path to “link course folder” is not efficient enough for a person who expects to get to work immediately. Heavy panel density slows the fast path.

**Jordan (First-Timer)**: The first-time scenario is the biggest risk. There is no strongly guided path for a user with zero vaults. “Add course folder” looks like secondary metadata instead of the primary action. That is exactly the sort of hidden prerequisite that causes abandonment.

**Sam (Study Multitasker)**: The product promises a connected study workspace, but the opening state still reads like a generic launcher. Users who want to compare material, research, and notes side by side are not being gently guided into the core workflow.

## Minor Observations

- The hero headline is strong, but “zero-telemetry document workstation” is a little more clever than clear. “Private local study workspace” is simpler and more trustworthy.
- The uppercase section labels are readable, but they also make the welcome screen feel more like a dashboard than a guided first-run flow.
- The recent files and bookmarks cards are useful, but they should not compete with the primary onboarding action.
- The app is directionally strong; the issue is not product identity, it is the sequencing of the first experience.

## Questions to Consider

- What is the single most important thing a student should do in the first 10 seconds after launch?
- Should the app default to a guided setup flow when it detects no course folder?
- Is the welcome view acting as a dashboard, a launcher, or a coach?
